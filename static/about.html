About TV Thing

Company Overview

TV Thing turns the Hauppauge HVR-1600 in viviserv into low-latency browser streams. The card carries two independent RF front-ends, so a digital and an analog channel stream at the same time, each on its own playlist. It is purpose-built around the installed hardware, with fast source changes and hard limits that keep a bad timestamp or encoder failure from running away with the machine. The same dial also carries YouTube TV through a Roku, streams from the web, and the station's own channels.

Technology Center

Why every tune starts fresh

Stations do not share one reliable timestamp clock. Reusing an encoder across channels can make FFmpeg invent huge runs of duplicate frames. Each request therefore gets a new source, encoder, stream ID, and segment namespace. The old picture stays visible until the new manifest is actually ready.

Quality presets

480p · 1 Mb/s720p · 2.5 Mb/sNative · 4.5 Mb/s

Audio is AAC at 96, 128, or 160 kb/s respectively.

Guardrails

  • Duplicate-frame and UDP-overrun trips
  • Maximum segment size and stalled-output watchdogs
  • CPU, memory, process, file-size, GPU, and temperature limits
  • Three real retunes plus software decode before a stream change ships

Encoding on viviserv

Production currently uses low-latency libx264. The NVIDIA Quadro K4200 can encode with NVENC, but sustained streaming drove this particular card to its thermal ceiling, so the safe CPU path is pinned. Hardware probing and automatic fallback remain available.

Operational pieces

server.py is both halves: control, and in the feed role (feed_server.py) the lanes. channels.py is the one list of channels that the lineup, catalog, guide and tune all read, and web_basic.py runs the web streams. dvb_source.py talks directly to Linux DVB, atsc_scan.py sweeps the band and probes single channels for DX, and analog_tuner.py discovers the card's tuner inputs and maps fixed US cable channel numbers onto its NTSC tuner, while stream_sanity_check.py measures request-to-stream time, decodability, segment behavior, write rate, CPU, memory, and GPU load, and web_stream_check.py watches a web stream's CPU and memory.

Two front-ends, two lanes

The S5H1409 ATSC demodulator and the analog tuner tune independently, so the digital and analog lanes each hold their own tuner, encoder, and HLS playlist and never displace one another. FM is the same analog tuner in radio demod mode, so it replaces analog TV only. The analog list exposes channels 1–135 from the fixed US cable STD plan and never runs a channel scan. Generic capture cards are not supported: discovery keeps only nodes that expose a V4L2 tuner input.

Scanning and DX

Channel scanning is native rather than a w_scan wrapper. Each frequency is visited once, reading the PSIP virtual channel table and the PAT/PMT together, with every PMT filtered concurrently. The sweep covers RF 2–36; everything above was reallocated by the 2020 repack. The DX meter probes one channel at a time and reports lock, signal, SNR and BER without disturbing the analog lane.

Tuner locks

A recorder or a set-top can claim a front-end so nothing retunes it mid-programme, either for a fixed time or until the guide says the current programme ends. A claim blocks tuning, stopping the lane, band scans and the scheduled guide refresh alike.

It deliberately does not block the always-on keeper. If the encoder behind a locked stream dies, the keeper still restarts that same channel — a lock whose stream cannot recover would be worse than no lock at all.

Holding the lock means holding the token issued with it; that token is what releases it early. Someone at this UI can override a lock anyway, but only through a prompt that names who holds it and what is about to be interrupted. Locks expire on their own and are capped, so a service that crashes cannot strand the tuner.

Where the guide comes from

Nothing is fetched from the internet. Every ATSC station transmits its own schedule, and epg.py reads it off the air with the same kind of section filter the channel scan uses: the Master Guide Table names the PIDs, the Event Information Tables carry the programmes, and the System Time Table supplies the leap-second offset that turns broadcast timestamps into real times.

The EIT arrives in three-hour blocks, and stations are only obliged to send four of them — so twelve hours is all the guide anyone is guaranteed. A single overnight refresh would run out by midday, which is why it runs twice: 03:00 and 15:00.

A refresh needs the digital tuner one frequency at a time, so it interrupts digital TV like a scan does. Tuning a channel takes the tuner straight back and the refresh keeps whatever it had read. Programme descriptions live in a separate table that repeats sixty times more slowly, so they are opt-in rather than automatic.

FM radio

The FM implementation remains in the project, but the control is hidden on viviserv because the installed HVR-1600 does not expose a usable FM audio path with the current Linux driver setup.

Our Standards: What a Change Must Pass

  • Every change: the fast test suite.
  • The stream stack (encoders, playlists, tuners and capture, process supervision, the feed): staged on isolated ports through three real retunes, then the same gate again on production.
  • Control alone: a restart under the drill, which fails if a stream, a recording or a relay so much as moved.
  • The web streams: ten minutes watched with no CPU excess or memory growth, and the video stopping once nobody watches.
  • The front door: a staged check, then a graceful reload.
  • Roku tuning and this site: the fast suite alone.
